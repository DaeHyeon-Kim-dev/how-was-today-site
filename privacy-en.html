How was today?

Privacy Policy

Effective 19 September 2026 · Last updated 19 September 2026

This version is written to the GDPR and the CCPA/CPRA. A separate document for the Republic of Korea (PIPA) is at 한국어판.

How was today? does not collect personal data. There is no server, there is no account, and the developer cannot read what you write.

1. Who is responsible for your data

The data controller for How was today? is Daehyeon Kim (individual developer).

Questions about privacy, or to exercise any right described below: kdhmonkeyd@gmail.com

2. EU representative

No EU representative has been appointed, because the developer does not receive or process any personal data from you.

Everything you write stays on your device and, if you enable it, in your own iCloud account. Article 27 GDPR requires a representative for controllers who process personal data of people in the EU; here no data reaches the developer to be processed.

3. What personal data we process

We process no personal data. The following are never received, stored or transmitted to the developer:

  • Name, email address, phone number, date of birth, gender
  • Address or location (the app never requests location access)
  • Contacts or photos (the app never requests access)
  • Device identifiers, advertising identifiers (IDFA), cookies
  • Payment details such as card numbers
  • Usage logs, analytics, crash reports

There is no sign-up and no developer-operated server. No analytics SDK (Google Analytics, Firebase or similar), no advertising SDK and no crash reporter is bundled with the app.

What you create — your one-line answers, an optional mood and weather, and the date — is content you hold, stored on your device and in your own iCloud. The developer has no copy and no way to obtain one.

4. Legal basis for processing

Because the developer processes no personal data, no legal basis under Article 6 GDPR is engaged for the developer.

Processing that does happen is carried out on your own device, under your control, to deliver the function you asked for:

iCloud sync uses Apple's CloudKit private database, which belongs to your Apple Account. The developer has no access to it. Turn iCloud off and the app still works; your entries then stay on the device alone.

6. When the app connects to the internet

The app makes no network requests of its own. There is no networking code in it and no developer server to talk to.

Two things use the network, and Apple handles both:

  • iCloud sync — between your own Apple Account storage and your own devices.
  • App Store purchases — the developer learns only whether this device has an active subscription.

Tapping the Terms of Use link on the purchase screen opens Apple's page in Safari. That is the same as typing the address into a browser; the app sends nothing.

7. Artificial intelligence

The app has no artificial intelligence features. Nothing you write is sent to a language model, on-device or otherwise.

8. Automated decision-making and profiling

We do not carry out automated decision-making that produces legal effects concerning you or similarly significantly affects you, within the meaning of Article 22 GDPR. We do not profile users.

There are no processors. The developer has no personal data to hand to anyone.

11. International transfers

The developer transfers no personal data anywhere, internationally or otherwise, because none is received.

If you enable iCloud, Apple stores your data in its own infrastructure and Apple is responsible for any international transfer, under its own safeguards. See the Apple Privacy Policy.

12. How long we keep data

The developer keeps nothing, so no retention period applies on the developer's side.

What you create stays on your device and in your iCloud until you delete it:

  • Delete everything — removing the app clears the on-device data. The iCloud copy is removed in iOS Settings → Apple Account → iCloud → Manage Storage.
  • Take it with you — Settings → Export writes your whole history to a text file. Reading and exporting stay open even after a subscription ends.

You do not need to ask the developer to delete anything; there is no copy to delete.

13. Security

  • There is no server, so there is no central store to breach.
  • Data is held under iOS device encryption and your Apple Account protections.
  • You can turn on App Lock so entries cannot be opened without Face ID or your passcode.
  • The diagnostic log records event names and character counts only — never the text of an answer or a question — and never leaves the device.

14. Personal data breaches

If a personal data breach occurs that is likely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms, we will notify the competent supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, as required by Article 33 GDPR, and will inform affected individuals where Article 34 requires it.

15. Children

This app is not directed at children and the developer knowingly collects no data from anyone, of any age.

Because nothing is transmitted or stored by the developer, no parental consent mechanism is required. Where local law sets an age of digital consent (16 in much of the EU), it is not engaged here.

18. California privacy rights

The developer collects no personal information, sells none and shares none — including for cross-context behavioural advertising. There is no category of personal information collected in the preceding twelve months, and therefore nothing to disclose, delete or opt out of.

You will never be discriminated against for exercising these rights, and no financial incentive is offered in exchange for personal information.
