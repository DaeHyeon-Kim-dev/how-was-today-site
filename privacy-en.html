How was today?

Privacy Policy

Effective 19 September 2026 · Last updated 26 September 2026

The English version, and the fallback for every region without its own. The app is not offered in the EU or EEA, so this is not a GDPR document. Also available in 한국어 and 日本語 and 繁體中文.

How was today? collects nothing. There is no server, there is no account, and the developer cannot read what you write.

1. Who is responsible

How was today? is made and operated by Daehyeon Kim, an individual developer.

For anything about privacy, write to kdhmonkeyd@gmail.com. You will get an answer as soon as it is read.

2. What we collect

We collect nothing. There is no account, no login, and no server operated by the developer. None of the following is ever received, stored or transmitted to us:

  • Name, email address, phone number, date of birth, gender
  • Address or location (the app never requests location access)
  • Contacts or photos (the app never requests access)
  • Device identifiers, advertising identifiers (IDFA), cookies
  • Payment details such as card numbers
  • Usage logs, analytics, crash reports

No analytics SDK (Google Analytics, Firebase or similar), no advertising SDK and no crash reporter is bundled with the app. Not one third-party SDK is included.

What you create — your one-line answers, an optional mood and weather, and the date — is yours. It lives on your device and, if you turn it on, in your own iCloud. The developer has no copy and no way to get one.

3. Where your data lives

iCloud sync uses Apple's CloudKit private database, which belongs to your Apple Account. The developer has no access to it. Turn iCloud off and the app still works; your entries then stay on the device alone.

4. When the app uses the internet

The app makes no network requests of its own. There is no networking code in it and no developer server to talk to.

Two things use the network, and Apple handles both:

  • iCloud sync — between your own Apple Account storage and your own devices.
  • App Store purchases — the developer learns only whether this device has an active subscription.

Tapping the Terms of Use link on the purchase screen opens Apple's page in Safari. That is the same as typing the address into a browser; the app sends nothing.

5. Artificial intelligence

This app has no artificial intelligence features. Nothing you write is sent to a language model, on-device or otherwise.

6. Device permissions

Declining a permission leaves the rest of the app working, and you can withdraw it at any time in iOS Settings. Location, contacts, photos, calendar, microphone and camera are never requested.

7. Payments

Purchases are handled by Apple's App Store. The developer never receives, sees or stores card numbers or payment details. All Apple reports back is whether this device has an active subscription.

Cancellations and refunds follow Apple's policy. You can cancel in Settings → Apple Account → Subscriptions.

8. Who else receives data

Nobody. We hold no personal data to pass on, and nothing is ever sold or shared.

The app does rely on Apple services, and those are a relationship between you and Apple:

ServiceUsed forTheir policy
iCloud (CloudKit)Sync between your devices, in your Apple AccountApple Privacy Policy

If law enforcement asked, there would be nothing to hand over. Your entries are on your device and in your Apple Account.

9. How long data is kept

The developer keeps nothing, so no retention period applies on our side.

What you create stays on your device and in your iCloud until you delete it:

  • Delete everything — removing the app clears the on-device data. The iCloud copy is removed in iOS Settings → Apple Account → iCloud → Manage Storage.
  • Take it with you — Settings → Export writes your whole history to a text file. Reading and exporting stay open even after a subscription ends.

You do not need to ask the developer to delete anything; there is no copy to delete.

10. Security

  • There is no server. There is no central store to breach.
  • Data sits under iOS device encryption and your Apple Account protections.
  • You can turn on App Lock so entries cannot be opened without Face ID or your passcode.
  • The diagnostic log records event names and character counts only — never the text of an answer or a question — and never leaves the device.

11. Children

How was today? is not directed to children under 13, and the developer knowingly collects no personal information from anyone, of any age. Because nothing is transmitted to or stored by the developer, there is no parental consent mechanism to operate under the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA).

If you believe a child has somehow provided personal information, write to kdhmonkeyd@gmail.com and it will be looked into.

12. California privacy rights

Under the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the CPRA, California residents may know, delete, correct, and opt out of the sale or sharing of their personal information.

The developer collects no personal information, sells none, and shares none — including for cross-context behavioural advertising. No category of personal information was collected in the preceding twelve months, so there is nothing to disclose, delete, or opt out of. No sensitive personal information is collected either.

You will never be treated differently for exercising these rights, and no financial incentive is offered in exchange for personal information. To ask anything, write to kdhmonkeyd@gmail.com.

13. For users in Japan

Under Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI), a business must disclose the purpose of use of personal information it handles, obtain consent before providing it to third parties, and respond to requests about retained personal data.

The developer handles no personal information. Nothing you write leaves your device for the developer, so there is no purpose of use to disclose, no third-party provision to consent to, and no retained personal data to disclose, correct, or delete. Requests can still be sent to kdhmonkeyd@gmail.com.

14. For users in the United Kingdom

Under the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, you have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, and objection, and the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

The developer is neither a controller nor a processor of your entries. What you write stays on your device and, if you leave iCloud on, in your own iCloud account, where Apple acts for you under your own agreement with Apple. There is no transfer of your entries to the developer and no international transfer by the developer, so there is nothing here for us to access, rectify, erase, restrict, port, or object to. You can read, export, and delete everything yourself inside the app. Questions are welcome at kdhmonkeyd@gmail.com.

15. For users elsewhere

Similar rights exist under Canada's PIPEDA, Brazil's LGPD, Australia's Privacy Act, India's DPDP Act, and comparable laws. The answer is the same in every case, because it follows from how the app is built rather than from where you live: the developer holds none of your data. There is no server, no analytics, and no account with us.

The two rights that matter in practice — getting your data out, and deleting it — are in the app itself, and they work without asking anyone. Settings → Export entries writes everything to a plain text file. Deleting an entry, or deleting the app and turning off iCloud for it, removes it.

16. Where this app is available

The app is not offered in the European Union or the European Economic Area. If you are in the EU or EEA, it should not appear on your App Store. This policy is written for the regions where the app is sold.

17. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the effective date at the top changes with it and the change is noted in the app's release notes.

If an analytics tool, a crash reporter, or any server feature is ever added, this policy is updated first and states plainly what goes where.
